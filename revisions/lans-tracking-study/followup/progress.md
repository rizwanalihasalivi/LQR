# Same-framework LANS follow-up

The benchmark is the tracking study with policy seeds 72–74 and physical seeds 26000–26004, matched reference scales and 14 conditions. The metric is mean failure-penalized RMSE against the unchanged limited PPO, fast LQR and objective-matched MPC episode results. The original policies and study results remain in their original directories.

## Diagnosis

For the 1650 kg nominal conditions, summing squared lateral error from the saved traces gives about 0.0110–0.0111 m² across the first 25 updates for LANS and 0.00184 m² for matched MPC, averaged per episode and excluding the initial state. For 1100 kg and 2200 kg DLC, LANS also accumulates 0.0263 and 0.0293 m² after update 25, respectively. The mass is available to the simulator and absent from the 11 actor inputs. The traces show a mass-dependent error pattern without establishing that adding mass would solve it.

The bounded, offline full-reference optimization on calibration seeds 22800–22802 found solutions only about 0.01% below matched MPC's mean penalized RMSE for the four nominal-mass conditions. The corresponding gaps for the two off-nominal mass conditions were about 0.6% and 0.8%. Those diagnostic solutions use exact mass and the entire reference. They establish that the observed comparison margin is small on those cases. They provide neither a causal deployment method nor a proof of optimality across scenarios.

## Reproducing the trials

Run from the tracking study directory, with the repository's Python 3.12 environment and single-threaded BLAS.

```bash

python followup/tune_head.py followup/calibration/matched_mpc.csv followup/replay_broad broad
python followup/score.py test followup/replay_broad followup/replay_broad
```

The broad search uses distinct calibration seeds 22800–22805 and two reference scales. It modifies only the final actor weight matrix and bias of each of the three existing policy seeds. It does not access test scores during search. The study uses the same gain, control composition, delay queue, simulator, and 11 observations.

The balanced, nominal, broad and blend searches and offline distillation trials each preserve their own checkpoints and results. The broad validation selection produced 8/14 first and 504/504 complete. On the original benchmark, the broad head update reached 7/14 first and 420/420 complete. It retained the five original wins and added mixed sine and mixed step. Mixed DLC remained second, with mean RMSE 0.52% above PPO. All six no-delay conditions remained behind matched MPC.

The original scalar evaluator reproduced the broad candidate's 7/14 result. An independent replay used physical seeds 22950–22954 and reference scales (0.90, 1.02) and (1.14, 0.94), with all three reference controllers rerun. The broad candidate ranked first in 8/14 there and completed 420/420. The benchmark still stands at 7/14. The blend candidate had 6/14 first on the benchmark and 420/420 completions.

The same constrained offline diagnostic on the original benchmark narrowed the four 1650 kg nominal cases to a margin of 0.115–0.128% below MPC. The margins at 1100 kg and 2200 kg were 0.991% and 1.188%. Both sets of oracle diagnostics are saved. The more aggressive zero-regularization solve returned an inaccurate-solution status, so no result from that solve was used. Constrained-oracle distillation preserved all 84 validation completions at 400 and 1200 updates, with 1/14 first at each checkpoint. At 3200 updates it dropped to 69/84 completions and 0/14 first. None of those checkpoints qualified for the benchmark.

The final worst-delay-margin head search reached 8/14 on broad calibration. It reduced aggregate error but had 5/14 first on both the original benchmark and independent replay, with 420/420 completions on each. The broad candidate remains the retained follow-up by first-place count.

## Initial-transient lower bound

Run `python followup/nominal_bound.py` to reproduce the bounded least-squares calculation for the first 40 no-delay updates of every original benchmark case. For each initial state, the calculation grants the optimizer exact mass and reference and any steering sequence within ±0.5 rad. It drops future error after update 40 and imposes no terminal condition. A supporting hyperplane to the quadratic tracking objective gives a lower bound under the same steering constraints. The fixed initial error is included before applying the benchmark RMSE normalization and averaging episodes. This relaxation is for diagnosis and supplies no deployment inputs.

For the four 1650 kg cases, matched MPC is within 1.55–1.71 micrometers of this mean RMSE lower bound. A strict first place at that mass is confined to a window of 0.118–0.130% below MPC. The two off-nominal mass windows are wider, at 12.20 and 16.71 micrometers. These are upper limits on how much a feasible controller could improve over MPC on those specific cases, with no guarantee a causal controller can attain them.

## Mixed-delay follow-up

The mixed-delay search starts from the broad checkpoints. It fits output-head directions to state and observed latency features on the existing broad calibration split, with an explicit objective for mean and worst mixed-delay error and penalties for fixed-delay and nominal regressions. All eight broad calibration wins were required for candidate retention.


The selected candidate retained 8/14 first on calibration and completed all 504 calibration episodes. On the original benchmark it had 6/14 first and 420/420 complete. Mixed DLC remained second, with RMSE 0.697% above frozen PPO. Mixed step slipped to second, 0.364% above PPO. The unchanged independent replay gave 7/14 first and 420/420 complete. The broad candidate remains the best retained result at 7/14 benchmark and 8/14 independent replay.

The nominal DLC first-steering diagnostic (`python followup/shared_initial_action.py`) compared independent 40-update optimal steering sequences for the three benchmark masses to a joint fit with a shared first action. Each seed/reference pair has identical initial observations across masses. All ten pairs wanted the same saturated initial steering across masses. Forcing agreement had no measurable prefix squared-error penalty. This rules out a mass-dependent first-action conflict on these cases. It leaves subsequent transient control and later tracking error as open questions.

Replaying the retained broad policies over the first 40 nominal DLC updates exposed a large actual first-command gap even though the offline solution agrees across masses. The retained policy's mean absolute first steering is 0.02445 rad, compared with 0.5 rad for the bounded offline solution, with mean absolute command difference 0.47555 rad in each mass group. The command differences drop to 0.00142–0.00649 rad at update 20. This identifies a weak early control response in the retained policy. The same stronger action cannot be assumed to preserve delayed behavior because the initial observed delay can be zero under both nominal and delayed protocols. These benchmark traces are diagnostic only.

## Initial-transient tuning

The transient mode starts from the retained broad checkpoints and fits additional actor-head directions from existing state and observed-delay channels during larger lateral errors. Its calibration objective gives priority to nominal RMSE and requires retention of all eight existing calibration wins and all 504 episodes.

```bash

python followup/steering_gap.py followup/replay_transient followup/replay_transient/nominal_dlc_steering_gap.csv
```

The candidate reduced original-benchmark nominal RMSE by 32.31% for slalom, 5.53% for 2200 kg DLC, and 2.79% for 1650 kg DLC compared with the broad candidate. The 1100 kg DLC nominal error rose 3.65%. Nominal DLC first steering magnitude moved only from 0.02445 to 0.02486 rad on average. All six nominal conditions were still behind MPC. The candidate lost mixed step on the benchmark and placed first in 6/14 with mean rank 1.643. It placed first in 8/14 on independent replay with mean rank 1.500. Both runs completed all 420 episodes. The broad checkpoints remain the benchmark leader by first-place count.

The first-command-conditioned diagnostic (`python followup/first_action_bound.py`) fixes the retained broad policy's actual first steering command in every nominal benchmark episode, relaxes subsequent steering to any bounded sequence, and computes the same 40-update lower bound. Every one of the six mean RMSE bounds exceeds matched MPC. The excess is roughly 13.5–17.6%. Consequently, changing only the subsequent actions while keeping those first commands cannot win any nominal condition on this benchmark. A viable candidate needs a materially different initial response, followed by a delayed-case replay to check the trade-off.

It interpolates the retained actor's residual action toward saturated steering opposite the observed lateral error only at the first update, then resumes the unmodified retained policy. The joint recovery mode (`python followup/tune_head.py followup/calibration/matched_mpc.csv followup/replay_recovery recovery`) starts from the retained eight-win actor and adds output-head directions for both stronger reset steering and recovery when delay is observable. ## Direct FIFO transient training

`direct_fifo.py` differentiates 120 simulated updates for each delay regime on training seeds 22522–22524. It uses the original command release-generation map, the causal observed-delay channels, the same saturation, and the same gain. Losses are normalized by source early-trajectory errors in each regime; the delayed losses receive an added penalty for regressions. An action-distance regularizer remains in the objective. The original scalar replay runs untouched.

The 25, 60, and 100 update snapshots each achieved eight calibration wins and 504/504 completions. The 25-update snapshot had the lowest full-episode calibration RMSE. Its replays gave 8/14, 8/14, and 5/14 first on benchmark, independent, and confirmation, all at 420/420 completion. Whole-episode RMSE was slightly higher than the retained actor on all three replays. It gained no no-delay win and did not recover the three mixed-delay confirmation losses. The checksum-pinned retained candidate stays in place.

With `direct_fifo.py --full-episode`, differentiable training covers all 1000 updates on separate seeds 22525–22527. The command gather uses a window determined by the maximum generation age and preserves the original FIFO indices. All snapshots won 8/14 on calibration and completed 504/504. The 35-update snapshot reduced aggregate calibration penalized RMSE by 17.1% relative to the retained actor and was selected for replay. It achieved 8/14, 8/14, and 5/14 first on the benchmark, independent, and confirmation splits, with 420/420 completions each. Geometric mean 200 ms RMSE fell by 22.74%, 19.97%, and 25.04%, respectively. Nominal RMSE rose by 2.45%, 1.28%, and 3.73%, and no additional first places were gained. This checkpoint is saved as a fixed-delay improvement trial. The checksum-pinned retained policy still has stronger nominal tracking.

The full-episode nominal-priority variant (`direct_fifo.py --nominal-priority`) credits nominal and mixed improvement, removes the reward for further fixed-delay improvement, and penalizes any delayed regression above the source loss. The 8, 20, and 35 update snapshots each retained all eight delayed calibration wins and completed 504/504. Each increased RMSE in all six nominal calibration conditions relative to the retained checkpoint. Geometric mean nominal RMSE rose 0.71%, 0.97%, and 1.10%, respectively. ## Joint actor recovery and nominal refinement

`joint_actor_recovery.py` fits the existing hidden and output weights using nominal-oracle labels on no-delay rollouts and retained-policy labels on delayed rollouts, including delayed states reached after stronger reset commands. It omits conflicting delayed reset labels from the first two trials. The base trial completed 287, 308, and 347 of 504 calibration episodes at 500, 1500, and 4000 updates. Aggregating the joint actor's own rollout states on fresh seeds 22564–22568 raised completion to 432, 462, and 458. The best completion snapshot finished 216/216 nominal, 142/144 mixed, and 104/144 fixed-delay episodes. None of the snapshots won a calibration condition.

The observed-teacher variant makes training labels a function of existing delay observations, blending toward the retained actor as previous and recent delay rise. It uses no extra deployment switch. On separate training seeds 22575–22579, calibration completion climbed from 216 to 226 of 504 and no condition ranked first. None of the joint-actor snapshots advanced to benchmark replay.

The two direct nominal refinements (`direct_fifo.py --nominal-polish` and `--nominal-rmse`) start from the nominal-oracle 2400-step actor. The first normalizes each nominal condition's full-episode squared-error loss, and the second uses mean per-episode RMSE including the initial error. On calibration the first reached 1.003–1.032 times matched MPC's nominal RMSE at 48 updates. The refined snapshots won no conditions and completed 216/216 nominal episodes and 0/288 delayed episodes. Saturated reset commands alone did not close the gap: the starting nominal-oracle actor already commanded at the 0.5 rad limit in 213/216 nominal calibration episodes. Slalom and 1100 kg DLC had the largest remaining nominal RMSE gaps.

A candidate-only numeric sweep of the existing exponential gain decay used 0.008, 0.016, and 0.030 per ms with the nominal-oracle actor. All three settings finished 216/216 nominal and 0/144 mixed episodes, with fixed-delay completions rising from 0 to 75 and then 126 of 144. None won a condition. Each run records its candidate parameter separately. The gain formula, observations, FIFO, and frozen references were kept.

The steering-Jacobian transfer (`anchor_projected_transfer.py`) projects nominal imitation updates onto the approximate null space of steering Jacobians at 96 sampled delayed observations per step. It starts from the checksum-pinned actor and uses the polished nominal actor as an offline target on fresh training seeds 22580–22583. Local anchor drift stayed small at the logged snapshots, but steering changes accumulated elsewhere. At 30, 120, and 300 updates calibration first places were 8, 6, and 3, respectively, and completions were 504, 504, and 486. The 30-step candidate lowered nominal calibration RMSE 2.34% and advanced. It achieved 8/14, 8/14 and 5/14 on benchmark, independent, and confirmation replays, with 420/420 completions each. It gained no no-delay first place. The retained actor remains preserved.

The 30-step steering-Jacobian transfer was verified with the repository's original scalar evaluator in an isolated copy. Its eight benchmark wins are strict, and the original evaluator confirmed 420/420 completions. Frozen comparator RMSEs matched byte-for-byte as CSV field strings. The nominal geometric-mean RMSE improvement over the checksum-pinned source was 4.21% on the official benchmark, 2.44% on independent replay, and 6.23% on confirmation. All six nominal cases remained behind MPC.

The compact-context initialization (`actor_context_init.py`) fits smaller networks offline, then encodes their delay-conditioned responses in the weights of one existing 64×64 actor using its two observed delay channels. There is no extra deployment input or external switch. Its 1500- and 4500-update snapshots completed 349 and 355 of 504 calibration episodes and won no conditions. Neither qualified for replay.

The scaled-delay curriculum (`delay_curriculum.py`) trained the same actor on 8%, 20%, 45%, 70%, and 100% of the sampled delay schedules on separate seeds 22588–22591. The release-generation map was reconstructed for each training level. All calibration snapshots used the full original evaluation delays. All finished 216/216 nominal and 0/288 delayed episodes with zero wins; none qualified for replay.

The cumulative-anchor correction (`anchor_projected_transfer.py --correct-anchor`) uses a bounded least-squares correction toward the retained steering output on the sampled delayed observations. In calibration it kept all eight delayed wins at 40, 120, and 300 updates, each with 504/504 completions. The 300-update candidate gave the largest nominal calibration improvement (2.78%). It had 8/14, 8/14 and 5/14 strict first places on benchmark, independent and confirmation, respectively, with 420/420 completions each. Nominal RMSE decreased by 5.61%, 3.20% and 7.66% relative to the checksum-pinned source. The original scalar evaluator independently reproduced the eight strict benchmark wins against the identical frozen comparator values. All six nominal conditions remain behind matched MPC.

The refreshed-anchor pass (`anchor_projected_transfer.py --refresh-anchors`) starts from the corrected 300-step model, samples the current actor's delayed trajectories on fresh seeds 22592–22595, and anchors outputs to the original checksum-pinned actor. It completed 504/504 calibration episodes and held eight wins at 120, 300, and 900 updates. Its nominal calibration improvement over the pinned actor dropped to 1.83%, 0.70%, and -1.73%, respectively. All three were worse on that metric than the starting corrected model, so the refresh did not advance to replay.

The closed-loop steering-anchor trial (`direct_fifo.py --anchor-nominal`) starts from the corrected 300-step model and directly differentiates nominal RMSE over 360 updates using fresh physical seeds 22596–22599. It uses the unchanged delayed observations to compute steering Jacobian projections and a bounded correction to the original retained policy. All snapshots held eight calibration wins with 504/504 completions. The 24-update snapshot gave the largest nominal calibration improvement over its source (0.43%). It achieved 8/14, 8/14, and 5/14 on benchmark, independent, and confirmation with 420/420 completions each. The original evaluator separately verified the eight strict benchmark wins and identical frozen comparator RMSE values. The nominal geometric-mean improvement over the pinned 0.10 blend is about 6.45% on the official benchmark. Matched MPC still leads all six nominal cases. Earlier verified checkpoints, the independent replay, and the confirmation evidence are preserved.

## Phase-resolved benchmark errors and Gauss–Newton fitting

`nominal_phase_errors.py` compares the retained joint and polished nominal-only actors with the frozen MPC traces. It reports mean per-episode RMSE and the mean summed squared lateral errors over updates 1–10, 11–40, and 41–1000. The first 10 updates explain 80.4–83.5% of the polished nominal-only actor's excess squared error across the four 1650 kg cases. At 1100 kg, 96.6% of the excess appears after update 40. The 2200 kg case has excess early error and slightly smaller late error than MPC. It is appropriate to target different phases by condition during training. The split and metric used for ranking are unchanged.

`gauss_newton_actor.py` propagates local head-weight sensitivities through the sampled plant and FIFO, then uses a damped least-squares proposal and closed-loop line search. The analytic derivative treats preview quantization as a straight-through cast. A cross-check against scalar trajectories found approximate batched RMS differences of 0.0000006, 0.0000033, and 0.00059 m for one nominal, mixed, and 200 ms training-independent probe. The batched fit is a training tool. Ranks and completions are measured through scalar replays.

The nominal-only 4-, 12-, and 24-step snapshots all completed the 216 nominal calibration episodes and won no condition. The 4-step snapshot had essentially the same nominal calibration RMSE as the source (0.013% lower) and the later snapshots regressed. No nominal-only candidate moved to benchmark replay. The joint variant starts from the verified closed-loop actor and guards delay conditions separately. All three snapshots kept eight calibration wins and 504/504 completions. The 12-step snapshot had the best nominal calibration improvement and advanced to the three scalar replay splits.

The joint solver's 12-step snapshot achieved 8/14 benchmark, 8/14 independent, and 5/14 confirmation, all with 420/420 completions. The repository evaluator verified eight strict benchmark wins and identical frozen comparator fields in the official table. Compared with the source, geometric mean benchmark RMSE decreased 1.49% nominally, 4.20% in mixed delay, and 11.68% at 200 ms. The unchanged architecture and all retained evidence remain in place.

The phase-weighted follow-up allocated more training error weight to the first 10 updates for 1650 kg and later updates for off-mass DLC. Its 4- and 12-step snapshots completed 504/504 and kept eight calibration wins; the 24-step set completed 498/504 and kept seven. Although the 24-step snapshot cut 1100 kg DLC nominal RMSE 1.29% relative to the starting joint actor, slalom nominal RMSE rose 4.76%, so none advanced to replay. The condition-protected follow-up explicitly caps each condition rather than letting that trade-off disappear in an average.

`nominal_oracle_actor.py --precision` uses clipped steering error, emphasizes the diagnosed error phases, and trains on independent seeds 22638–22649 at three reference scales. The calibration snapshots at 500, 1500, and 4000 updates won zero, one, and two nominal conditions. At 4000 updates, 1100 kg and 2200 kg DLC passed the frozen MPC on calibration and the other four nominal RMSEs were 0.17–0.29% higher. All snapshots completed 216/216 nominal and 0/288 delayed calibration episodes. These are nominal-only diagnostic wins. They do not count toward the verified benchmark goal.

The margin-guarded second-order fit (`gauss_newton_actor.py --margin-guard`) solves a damped head update with separate linearized constraints for delayed training RMSE, then checks full simulated trajectories before accepting. The ceiling is tied to the source and the earlier pinned actor, so part of the newly earned delayed margin can be used for nominal improvement. The 24-step set won 8/14, 8/14, and 5/14 on the three replay splits, all with 420/420 completions. Nominal RMSE fell 2.13%, 1.84%, and 2.80% relative to the preceding second-order joint model. The benchmark mixed and fixed-delay geometric means rose 1.06% and 5.68%, so both checkpoint sets remain available. No additional condition ranked first.

The condition-protected variant adds separate nominal ceilings, including a cap on slalom regression, to the same training fit. Only its 4-step snapshot retained all eight calibration wins. It completed 504/504 and cut nominal calibration RMSE 1.67% relative to the source; the 12- and 24-step snapshots kept seven wins and were not selected.

`delay_channel_recovery.py` updates only the first layer's two existing delay-input columns. All 216 no-delay calibration episode rows were exactly identical between the precision source and each of the three trained snapshots. Their two nominal calibration wins were retained. At most two of the 288 delayed calibration episodes completed with the default gain, so none qualified for benchmark replay.

`queue_visibility.py` measures pending commands that are older than the actor's observable delay-history window. In calibration, independent, and confirmation schedules, 0.74–0.98% of mixed ticks had a previous release still pending, both observed delay channels zero, and the new command's gain unattenuated. Every sampled physical mixed episode contains such a tick. The queue schedule is independent of steering. This shows why changing only delay-input columns cannot act directly on those observations. It does not establish that all such ticks cause an episode failure.

The condition-protected 4-step snapshot also retained 8/14, 8/14, and 5/14 on scalar benchmark, independent, and confirmation replays with 420/420 each. It gained no nominal first place. The original repository replay for the joint 12-step snapshot remains the independently verified result for this solver family.

The nominal-anchored recovery (`nominal_anchored_recovery.py`) warms from delay-column recovery and explicitly upweights training samples with the queue-visibility gap. It projects delayed-steering updates away from sampled nominal command gradients. At 200 updates it preserved one nominal calibration win, and later snapshots preserved zero. All three completed 216 nominal and zero delayed calibration episodes. No candidate advanced.

A candidate-only decay sweep on the delay-column actor tested 0.012, 0.025, and 0.040 per ms. The two nominal calibration wins were identical in each setting. The 0.025 setting completed 126 fixed-delay and one mixed episode out of 288 delayed runs, without a delayed first place. The gain formula and evaluation comparators were unchanged.

`steering_authority.py` checks which verified steering commands remain attainable at hypothetical decay settings while holding the recorded states and LQR gain fixed. At 200 ms and decay 0.025, 96.46% of the verified joint actor's slalom commands were outside the allowed interval. At 0.008, every recorded command was attainable in all four cases. This is an authority diagnostic on the sampled trajectories, not a stability guarantee for a new policy.

The early-refinement pass (`nominal_oracle_actor.py --early-refine`) emphasizes the first ten 1650 kg updates and uses smaller offline expert regularizers on separate training seeds. All snapshots kept the two nominal calibration wins and completed only the 216 nominal cases. The 4000-step nominal geometric mean was 0.027% worse than the preceding precision actor, and no additional win appeared. None advanced to benchmark replay.

The gain-protected curriculum (`delay_curriculum.py --gain-protected`) trained with the 0.025 coefficient at increasingly long sampled delays. All calibration snapshots completed 342/504, slightly below the source sweep at 343/504. The 64-step set kept two nominal wins and the later sets kept one; none won a delayed condition. The intermediate-gain curriculum (`--intermediate-gain`) reduced the training coefficient from 0.025 to 0.008 while increasing delays. Calibration at 0.008 completed 216, 217, and 216 of 504 at its three snapshots. At most two nominal wins and zero delayed wins remained. The lower decay restores steering authority, but this curriculum did not teach the mixed and fixed-delay recovery needed to use it. Neither trial advanced to the benchmark.

The bias-augmented second-order fit (`gauss_newton_actor.py --fit-bias`) updates the first-layer biases and existing output head together, with condition-specific guards. Its 12-step candidate preserved eight calibration wins with 504/504 and gave the larger nominal improvement. The three replays measured 8/14, 8/14, and 5/14 strict first places, all with 420/420. Relative to the preceding margin-guarded set, no-delay geometric means decreased 0.98%, 1.28%, and 1.21%. Delayed error changed slightly, with no change in wins. All earlier checkpoints are still retained.

The joint delay-column extension (`gauss_newton_actor.py --fit-delay`) starts from the bias-augmented 12-step policy and includes the original first layer's existing delay-input weights in the local fit alongside its biases and output head. The 3- and 8-step snapshots finished all 504 calibration episodes but ranked first in seven conditions. Neither advanced to replay. The previously verified joint checkpoint sets and the two nominal-only calibration wins remain saved separately.

## Prefix-locked diagnosis and receding nominal labels

`prefix_lock_diagnosis.py` fixes 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, or 40 actual commands from a checkpoint on each nominal calibration episode. It optimizes the remaining commands over the first 40 updates subject to the original steering limit and drops all later error, so it gives a lower bound on whole-episode RMSE. Keeping only the retained joint actor's first command already raises the lower bound 9.9–13.1% above frozen MPC across all six conditions. The precision nominal-only actor's first command leaves their lower bounds below MPC, but freezing the first two commands makes the four 1650 kg bounds exceed MPC. The new receding-target actor keeps DLC, sine, and step just below the bound after its second command and above it by its fifth. Slalom still crosses at the second command. The two off-mass bounds remain below MPC.

`receding_nominal_targets.py` replaces clipped unconstrained off-policy recovery targets with bounded, 28-step QP commands recomputed from each sampled state. It uses exact mass and future reference only in offline label generation. OSQP reported inaccurate solves on some states. The validated bounded least-squares fallback solves the identical factored quadratic and steering bounds, and completed 43–48 fallback solves per seed in the finished training runs. The original evaluator and frozen comparator were not altered.

`receding_teacher_check.py` replays the offline teacher itself on the six calibration conditions and all 72 completed. It beats frozen MPC in all six, with a margin of only about 0.21–0.30 micrometers of RMSE for the nominal-mass conditions. The learned 5000-step nominal actor retained the two off-mass calibration wins. Its four nominal-mass RMSE ratios were 1.00062–1.00125 relative to MPC. It still completed only nominal episodes, so it is not a joint benchmark candidate.

The joint prefix-focused transfer (`anchor_projected_transfer.py --prefix-focus`) starts from the verified joint Gauss–Newton actor and oversamples the first three bounded targets, with delayed output anchors. Its 150-step snapshot kept eight wins and 504/504 on calibration. It lowered no-delay geometric mean RMSE by 1.52% on the benchmark, 1.88% on independent replay, and 1.64% on confirmation compared with the source. Win counts were 8/14, 8/14, and 5/14, all with 420/420. Stronger 450- and 900-step snapshots lowered nominal calibration RMSE by 3.91% and 6.86% but lost four and twelve fixed-delay slalom episodes respectively. All of those failures occurred at the faster, larger slalom scale.

`repair_fixed_slalom.py` applies the closed-loop Gauss–Newton training fit only to the two existing delay-input columns, targeting the failed 200 ms slalom and guarding other delayed conditions. The first 900-step repair cut failed slalom calibration episodes from 12 to six. All six remaining failures used policy seed 73 and the same fast reference scale. It did not qualify for benchmark replay. The no-delay input values remain zero, so this parameter subset leaves nominal steering and the offline actor architecture intact.

Phase profiling found two clusters for 900-update fixed-delay slalom failures, at 1.55–1.63 seconds and 5.05–5.75 seconds. The original delay-column repair recovered the late cluster. The stress training pass added faster slalom references on independent seeds 22750–22757. The 900-update source still left six calibration failures; the 450-update source advanced to 503/504 completions, leaving one fast slalom failure. Its mean penalized RMSE for the condition was 0.25786 m versus 0.25635 m for PPO. The same no-delay steering weights were retained throughout. Candidate-only decay sweeps at 0.0025 and 0.003 per ms on the repaired 900-update source completed 498 and 496 calibration episodes and kept seven and five wins, respectively. The first-two-seconds objective on fresh seeds 22780–22787 also kept seven wins with 498/504 across all three snapshots.

`receding_prefix_refine.py` projects the first-eight-command improvement away from sampled later and off-mass steering gradients. Adding 96 independent reset seeds cut the remaining 1650 kg nominal RMSE gaps against frozen MPC to 0.028–0.067% at 1600 steps, versus 0.062–0.125% for the receding-label source. Both off-mass DLC conditions ranked first in calibration; these are nominal-only snapshots and completed zero of the 288 delayed episodes. They did not qualify for benchmark replay.

The extended stress pass weighted the worst quartile of fixed-200 ms slalom errors on fresh seeds 22788–22795. All three snapshots recovered eight calibration wins. They still completed 503/504, with one remaining failure for policy seed 73 on the high-rate slalom. Blending 10%, 25%, 50%, or 75% of the stable 150-update actor's delay columns into the 450-update stressed actor kept seven wins and completed 502, 502, 501, and 500 of 504. These sets missed the joint replay gate.

The stock evaluator reproduced the 150-update prefix actor's eight strict wins and 420/420 benchmark completions. The frozen PPO, LQR, and MPC RMSE and completion fields match the earlier official replay exactly. The 150-update checkpoint kept all eight delayed original benchmark wins and improved nominal RMSE without another first place.

`reset_delay_ambiguity.py` compared identical physical resets with and without fixed 200 ms delay. The 11 observed values matched exactly across all 144 paired starts. A single counterfactual reset command from the bounded nominal teacher worsened the fixed-delay DLC, sine, and step errors at both tested reference scales and the fast slalom error slightly. All 144 fixed-delay episodes still completed. This isolates an initial command trade-off; the intervention alone did not reproduce the premature slalom terminations.

The smaller-step continuation of the wide-reset nominal actor kept the two off-mass calibration wins and all 216 nominal completions. At 3200 updates, matched MPC still led the four 1650 kg conditions by 0.026–0.060% of its RMSE. The delayed completion count remained zero. The reduction in nominal error did not add a win.
